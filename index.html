PROBLEM → DESIGN → TRUST

Distinguish course completion from demonstrated learning.

The product should not force every course into the same assessment model. It should show partners the strength and limits of the evidence available, then offer a consistent, structured way to collect more evidence where needed.

1. Completion recordConfirms the learner met the platform's existing completion rule. It does not, by itself, prove competence.
2. Evidence layerConnects quiz results, proctored exams, projects, and a structured timed interview to the certificate.
3. Trust outcomeCommunicates evidence strength, gaps, course version, and review status instead of presenting a binary badge as certainty.

Product rules

  • Courses with no assessment receive a completion-only / limited-evidence outcome—not a competence claim.
  • Existing certificates are not silently revoked. They retain their historical completion status and can be labeled “legacy — evidence not assessed under the current standard.”
  • Revised courses get versioned standards. Old certificates are evaluated against the version completed, not automatically against a new curriculum.
  • Corporate learners use the same evidence rules. Lower engagement is not itself proof of poor learning; account context is disclosed separately.
  • Screen sharing, if used in a real deployment, requires explicit consent, a clear explanation, minimum necessary collection, and an alternative review path. The prototype does not capture a screen.
  • AI usage is evaluated by disclosure, understanding, attribution, and ability to explain or adapt the answer—not by treating all AI assistance as cheating.
  • Scores are decision support, not a guarantee of workplace performance. Employers should combine evidence with role-relevant interviews or work samples.

Proposed candidate flow

1. Identity & rulesOpen a time-limited link, check browser/session rules, explain permitted tools, and obtain consent.
2. Three sectionsSection 1: soft skills and work experience. Sections 2–3: specialty knowledge and applied scenarios.
3. Transparent scorecardReport soft skills, technical evidence, and answer reliability/tool-dependence separately, with evidence and reviewer notes.
MVP boundary: Start with certificate verification, evidence mapping, 3-section timed assessment, reviewer rubric, and a partner-facing result. Defer full LMS integration, automated proctoring, and high-confidence AI detection. Browser and AI-detection signals are imperfect and should never be presented as proof by themselves.
